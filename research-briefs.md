# Deep-research briefs — entry questions

Scope agreed 2026-10-12. Three separate runs, in order 1 → 2 → 3, then a short synthesis pass to turn the outputs into the actual 4-turn question script.

Shared context for all three: entry is ~3-4 exchanges of reflection + one focused question, feeding a visual inner landscape (parts with tentative identity, words, role, influence/size, position/relationships, confidence). Hybrid app role: start as Mirror (purely reflective), earn the right to become Gentle guide (small reversible experiments). All interpretations are editable hypotheses; tone is warm, calm, non-clinical.

The Mirror → Gentle guide transition does not get its own research run. It is a synthesis decision informed by all three briefs; explicit transition questions live in brief 3.

## Brief 1 — IFS elicitation

> Deep research: how to elicit inner parts through short written conversation without leading, diagnosing, or acting as a therapist.
>
> Context: a visual IFS-inspired app. Entry is ~3-4 exchanges; each app turn = short reflection + one focused question. Early turns stay purely reflective (Mirror). Output must feed a visual landscape: parts with tentative identity, words, emotional role.
>
> Questions: (1) What IFS / parts-work language reliably helps people externalize parts (naming, quoting inner voices, noticing Self-energy vs. blended states)? (2) What question forms elicit roles and burdens without implanting them? (3) What must a non-therapist app avoid asking or claiming? (4) What are good first-two-question openers for strangers to inner work?
>
> Prioritize: IFS introductory literature, expressive-writing and affect-labeling research, peer-support / non-clinical framing guidelines.
>
> Deliver: 5-8 safe elicitation patterns with example questions, plus anti-patterns (leading, diagnostic, over-authoritative) to avoid.

## Brief 2 — Mappable UX

> Deep research: how to turn plain-language answers into spatial attributes a visual landscape can render.
>
> Context: same app as above. Conversation must yield, in ~3-4 turns: each part's influence/size, centrality/closeness, relationships (allied, in tension, silenced), and who is unheard. Questions must feel natural, not like a form. Later turns may invite small reversible experiments (Gentle guide).
>
> Questions: (1) How do you ask about bigness, loudness, closeness, and being pushed aside in everyday words? (2) What existing tools map feelings/parts to space, and what wording do they use? (3) How to sequence questions so each turn adds one mappable layer without overwhelming? (4) How to confirm a mapping ("sounds like the critic is loudest — right?") without leading?
>
> Prioritize: conversational UX for sensitive topics, mood-mapping / sociometry / parts-mapping tools, progressive-disclosure and scaffolding patterns.
>
> Deliver: a turn-by-turn attribute plan (turn 1→4: what to elicit each turn) with example questions, plus anti-patterns (form-like interrogation, double-barreled questions).

## Brief 3 — Uncertainty framing + Mirror→Guide transition

> Deep research: how to present AI interpretations of inner life as tentative, editable hypotheses, and when to shift from reflecting to gently guiding.
>
> Context: same app. All generated parts are starting hypotheses the user can rename, correct, remove, or reinterpret. The app must distinguish "how things are now" from "how you'd like them to be" when the user moves/resizes characters. Tone: warm, calm, non-clinical; never villainize a part; never use darkness/menace for difficult parts.
>
> Questions: (1) What hedging and attribution phrasing ("it sounds like…", "one way to see this…") preserves agency while staying readable? (2) How do you show low confidence without undermining trust? (3) What UI + language patterns separate current-state edits from desired-state experiments (modes, prompts, before/after)? (4) When has an app "earned the right" to suggest a small experiment — what signals readiness, and how should the invitation be worded so it feels optional? (5) How to respond when the user corrects or rejects an interpretation?
>
> Prioritize: uncertainty communication in AI, therapeutic-alliance / readiness signals, undo-friendly and editable-AI interaction patterns.
>
> Deliver: a phrasing toolkit (hedges, corrections, experiment invitations), a current-vs-desired pattern recommendation, and transition criteria for Mirror → Gentle guide with example turns.
