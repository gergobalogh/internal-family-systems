## Answers to the brief's four questions

### 1. What IFS / parts-work language reliably helps people externalize parts?

- **User-owned naming.** "The first step in IFS therapy is helping clients
  identify and name their various internal parts… By naming these parts,
  clients begin to differentiate them from their core Self." (S1)
- **The "a part of me" shift.** "That tiny linguistic shift, from 'I am
  anxious' to 'a part of me is anxious,' opens up just enough distance to
  observe the emotion rather than be swallowed by it." (S2) Atlas's version:
  say "'the me' feels angry" — "you are not your anger." (S3)
- **Parts language is caught, not taught.** Therapists model it ("a part of
  you seems…") and clients adopt it; the app should reflect in parts language
  and let the user correct or rename.
- **Toward-check as Self-energy probe.** "How do you feel *toward* it? If the
  answer is curiosity, warmth, or openness, Self is present… If frustration,
  judgment, or the urge to make it go away, another part has stepped in."
  (S2) Same question "brings out polarization" — "I can't stand it" reveals a
  second, blended part. (S3, citing Earley 2009)

### 2. What question forms elicit roles and burdens without implanting them?

- **Befriending questions** assume good intent without supplying content:
  "What do you want me to know? What are you afraid will happen if you stop?
  How long have you been doing this job? What are you protecting me from?"
  (S2) IFS premise: "all our parts have good intentions… attempting to
  protect the self or the system from harm." (S3)
- **Clean Language developing questions** reuse only the user's words —
  "clean because they do not unwittingly contaminate the client's experience
  with your metaphors and assumptions" (S6): "And what kind of [X] is that
  [X]?", "And is there anything else about [X]?", "And where/whereabouts is
  [X]?", "And that's [X] like what?", "And what happens next?" (S6)
- **Aim: minimize co-construction of content.** "Clean Language Interviewing
  aims to minimise the co-construction of the *content*" even though all
  questions frame. (S7, Tosey 2015)
- **Quoting the voice, not interpreting it.** "If this part had words, what
  would it say?" (S9) keeps authorship with the user.
- **Why writing-first works.** Affect labeling "reduces amygdala activation"
  immediately and "has long-term benefits" (S4); expressive writing shows "a
  small but reliable average benefit" across 146 RCTs (S5). The opening
  free-write is itself the first intervention.

### 3. What must a non-therapist app avoid asking or claiming?

From S8 (with S2's pacing caveat and S3's exile framing):

- **Never diagnose or grade severity.** "A trained peer listener… cannot
  determine whether you have a depressive disorder or how severe it is." No
  disorder words (depression, anxiety, trauma, ADHD) as labels for the user.
- **Never provide psychotherapy or manage crisis.** Cannot "provide
  psychotherapy, or manage a mental health emergency"; suicidal/deteriorating
  users need urgent help, not the app (S8).
- **Never dig for exiles / trauma origins.** Exiles "carry the intolerable
  pain" and protectors keep them out of awareness (S3); even guided tools
  must check in, slow down, stop — "If something feels like more than you can
  hold alone, that's the cue to pause and bring in a trained therapist."
  (S2) No "what childhood wound causes this?", no suggested forgotten trauma.
- **Never promise improvement.** "Cannot guarantee that symptoms will
  improve… Any service promising to cure… is making a promise the evidence
  does not support." (S8)

### 4. Good first-two-question openers for strangers to inner work

Recommended (each = reflection + ONE question, Mirror mode):

- **Opener 1 — label + user-owned name** (grounds: S4 affect labeling, S1
  naming, S5 uncensored writing): after the free-write, reflect one line,
  then ask *"If you had to give a name to the feeling that's loudest right
  now, what would you call it?"* Naming externalizes (S1) and downregulates
  (S4); the user authors the name, so nothing is implanted.
- **Opener 2 — toward-check** (grounds: S2, S3): *"And how do you feel toward
  that [their name] — curious about it, frustrated with it, or something
  else?"* One question that simultaneously tests Self-energy, detects a
  second polarized part ("I hate it"), and yields the first relationship
  datum for the landscape.
- **Backup opener 2 — clean developing** (grounds: S6): *"And what kind of
  [their word] is that?"* Zero new vocabulary introduced; pure elaboration.

## Deliverable: 7 safe elicitation patterns

1. **Free-write invitation.** "In your own words, what's weighing on you
   right now? No need to be organized." (S4, S5 — labeling + disclosure do
   work before any question is asked.)
2. **Parts-language reflection.** Mirror back with "a part of you" framing
   and let them correct it: "It sounds like a part of you is bracing for
   criticism — does that fit, or would you say it differently?" (S1, S2)
3. **User-owned naming.** "What would you call this part / this feeling?"
   (S1) Never supply the name first (see anti-pattern 1).
4. **Toward-check.** "How do you feel toward that [name] right now?"
   Curiosity/warmth → proceed; frustration/hate/fear → name the *second*
   part ("sounds like another part has strong feelings about this one").
   (S2, S3)
5. **Voice-quoting.** "If this [name] had words, what would it say — in its
   own voice?" (S9) Produces quotable part-speech for the landscape.
6. **Clean developing with their words.** "What kind of [their word] is
   that?" / "Is there anything else about [their word]?" / "Where do you
   notice [their word]?" (S6) — the last doubles as the first spatial datum
   (feeds Brief 2).
7. **Protective-intent befriending.** "What might this part be trying to do
   for you?" / "What is it afraid would happen if it stopped?" (S2, S3)
   Presupposes good intent, supplies zero content.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

| # | Anti-pattern | Why | Say instead |
|---|--------------|-----|-------------|
| 1 | Naming the part for them ("that's your inner critic") | implants identity; violates Clean non-contamination (S6, S7) | "What would *you* call it?" |
| 2 | Diagnostic labeling ("this sounds like anxiety / depression") | non-clinical scope forbids diagnosis (S8) | reflect plain words: "a tight, restless feeling" |
| 3 | Trauma excavation ("what past wound causes this?") | exile work needs a therapist + safety frame (S2, S3) | stay present: "what's it like right now?" |
| 4 | Leading confirmation ("so the critic is the loudest, right?") | implants + over-authoritative (S6) | "Is there anything else about it?" |
| 5 | Instructed befriending ("thank your critic — it's helping") | IFS trust is earned through dialogue, not assigned (S1, S2) | "What might it be trying to do for you?" |
| 6 | Promising outcomes ("naming it will make it fade") | evidence doesn't support guarantees (S8); benefits are small-on-average (S5) | "naming it often makes it a little easier to look at" |

## Caveats

- Effect sizes are modest: expressive writing's benefit is "small but
  reliable" (S5); affect labeling is well-replicated neurally but is
  regulation, not treatment (S4).
- IFS-specific claims rest on practitioner literature (S1–S3), not RCTs;
  S3's evidence-registry claims were not independently verified.
- The 6 F's list (S9) is confirmed against a Schwartz-1997-adapted
  worksheet (fetched 2026-10-12). The Earley *Self-Therapy* attribution is
  corroborated secondhand (sample-session summaries) but the primary Earley
  text was not opened. "If this part had words, what would it say?" remains
  snippet-sourced.
- Clean Language's "no contamination" is an aspiration: even clean questions
  "set frames, contain presuppositions" (S7) — hence pattern 4's correction
  invitation and editable interpretations.
